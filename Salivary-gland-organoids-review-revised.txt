Abstract

Salivary gland disorders span a pathological spectrum, from histologically diverse

malignancies to autoimmune epithelial dysfunction in Sjögren’s disease and poorly reversible

radiation-induced hypofunction, each representing a major unmet clinical need inadequately

modeled by conventional preclinical systems. Self-organizing salivary gland organoids offer a

physiologically relevant alternative. We first consider organoid generation from adult epithelial

and pluripotent stem cells, the WNT–R-spondin, fibroblast growth factor and neuregulin

signals sustaining long-term human culture, and assays validating structural and secretory

function. A recurring constraint is that human organoids remain ductal-dominant with

incomplete acinar maturation, although murine differentiation also varies across protocols; we

assess strategies aimed at improving functional maturity. We then examine disease modeling,

including patient-derived tumor organoids for subtype-specific drug screening and models of

Sjögren’s disease and radiation injury, while distinguishing established epithelial findings from

prospective immune– and stromal–epithelial systems. We evaluate organoids as quantitative

pharmacology platforms for botulinum neurotoxin potency testing and secretagogue screening.

Finally,   we   appraise    regenerative   strategies   encompassing    organoid-derived     cell

transplantation entering first-in-human evaluation, chemically reprogrammed epithelial

progenitors and targeted extracellular vesicles, as well as manufacturing, standardization and

regulatory barriers to clinical use.




Keywords: human salivary gland organoids, species differences, acinar maturation, patient-

derived organoids, precision medicine, immune–epithelial crosstalk, radiation injury, chemical

reprogramming, regenerative medicine, standardization

INTRODUCTION

The salivary glands sustain oral health by secreting 0.5–1.5 L of saliva each day through a

branched architecture of secretory acini and a ductal network that refines the ionic composition

of the primary fluid (Fig. 1)1. This output depends on the coordinated activity of serous and

mucous acinar cells, intercalated and striated duct cells, myoepithelial cells and a dense

neurovascular supply, and this interdependence becomes clinically consequential when it

fails2-4. Two disease burdens dominate. Radiotherapy for head and neck cancer, autoimmune

destruction in Sjögren's disease and age-related atrophy converge on irreversible hypofunction

and xerostomia, for which treatment remains palliative 5,6. Salivary gland malignancies, by

contrast, display the widest histological diversity of any organ, with more than twenty World

Health Organization (WHO)-recognized subtypes, each demanding individualized management7.

Underlying both is a research problem: the available models capture little of this biology. Two-

dimensional (2D) cultures lose glandular architecture, and available cell lines and animal

models incompletely capture disease heterogeneity and the therapeutic responses8,9.

       Organoids offer a way past these limits. As self-organizing three-dimensional (3D)

cultures that reconstruct tissue architecture, cellular composition and physiological function,

they have opened salivary gland development, disease and regeneration to experimental access

in a human-relevant setting10-12. The field has matured quickly. Long-term culture systems

established from adult murine and human salivary tissues 13-15 defined key niche requirements

and species-dependent properties; patient-derived tumor libraries16,17, stromal- and endothelial-

integrated developmental models18,19, patient-derived Sjögren’s disease organoids 20, and

progenitor-cell transplantation followed. That trajectory now spans preclinical transplantation,

chemically defined human organoids, clinical-grade manufacturing, and clinically annotated

biorepositories15,21-23, marking a transition from proof of concept toward clinical application.

The underlying evidence nevertheless spans murine developmental and preclinical models,



                                               4
human organoid cultures, and transplantation of human-derived cells into mouse recipients,

each with distinct implications for clinical translation.

          Existing reviews have largely viewed salivary gland organoids through a single lens,

whether as regenerative constructs for xerostomia, as substrates for bioengineering and

bioprinting, or as one tissue within broader surveys of oral and dental organoids 24-28. What has

been missing is an account that treats the organoid at once as a precision-oncology platform,

an autoimmune disease model, a quantitative pharmacology tool and a regenerative therapy,

and that confronts the constraint common to all four: human organoids often remain ductal-

dominant and show incomplete, protocol-dependent acinar maturation. This review provides

that synthesis. Because murine and human organoids diverge in ways that bear on every

application that follows, we annotate the species origin of key evidence throughout and

consolidate the detailed comparison in a dedicated section (Species Differences: Murine versus

Human Organoid Models).

          We organize it around three unresolved tensions. The first is developmental and

concerns why human organoids stall short of functional acinar maturation and whether niche

engineering and defined differentiation can close the gap. The second is architectural. Most

current systems are epithelial-only, omitting the stromal, immune and matrix compartments

that shape malignancy and Sjögren's disease and so limiting their fidelity as disease models.

The third is translational. Transplantable cell products, chimeric models and pharmacology

assays each demand manufacturing and regulatory frameworks the field is only beginning to

build. Across the sections that follow, we trace these tensions from organoid generation and

functional validation, through tumor and autoimmune disease modeling and pharmacological

application, to regenerative therapy and the standardization challenges that will decide clinical

impact.

PRINCIPLES OF ORGANOID GENERATION

Cell Sources

The starting cells shape what a salivary gland organoid can model, how reproducibly it forms,

and whether it can reach the clinic11. Three sources dominate, each suited to different questions.

Adult epithelial cells are the most accessible. Unlike the intestine, where LGR5 lineage tracing

defines a clear stem-cell hierarchy29, the salivary gland does not conform to a comparably

simple model; progenitor populations can generate both ductal and acinar descendants 30 and

show broader plasticity after severe injury or removal from the native niche 31. Establishment

and differentiation are therefore less predictable than in organs with defined stem

compartments9. Their compensating strengths are an intrinsic bias toward salivary lineages 13

and direct suitability for autologous therapy 15, though limited proliferative capacity and age-

related decline constrain both32.

       Pluripotent stem cells (PSCs), particularly induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), trade

that lineage bias for scale and reach. Derived from accessible somatic cells and effectively

unlimited in expansion33,34, they enable disease modeling that primary tissue cannot support,

particularly for genetic conditions and rare disorders where sampling is difficult. In Sjögren’s

disease, where diagnostic biopsies typically sample labial minor glands, iPSC-based

approaches could provide a route to modeling major-gland lineages without direct major-gland

sampling, although this remains a prospective application 35. They have been used to model

salivary viral tropism36 and hold promise for autoimmune systems35. PSC-based salivary-gland

generation uses distinct strategies, including directed differentiation toward salivary-gland

lineages from human iPSCs37 and conditional blastocyst complementation in mice 38. The

capacity of PSC-derived salivary models to reproduce mature adult gland function remains

approach-dependent and requires direct functional validation ³⁶–³⁸,⁴⁷,35,39, while their

infrastructure and extended timelines can complicate applications that need rapid



                                                6
turnaround39,40.

        Embryonic and fetal tissues serve as the developmental reference against which the

other sources are judged. Rich in highly proliferative progenitors that adults cannot supply 41,42,

they reproduce epithelial branching and acinar lineage development but not yet the full ductal

architecture seen in vivo43,44, and remain essential benchmarks for validating PSC

differentiation protocols, despite the ethical and availability constraints that limit their use 45,46.

In practice, the three sources are complementary. In murine studies, adult epithelium is often

used, and murine PSC-based approaches include conditional blastocyst complementation38 and

transcription-factor-guided organoid induction47. In human studies, cell sources are selected

according to the study aim, with patient tissue or iPSCs used for disease modeling and

embryonic tissue for development. Drawing on all three, each matched to the question it

answers best, gives the most complete platform (Table 1).



Signaling Pathways and Niche Factors

Salivary gland organoids depend on faithfully reconstructing the cellular niche through a

balanced set of signals, whose interplay sets the conditions for formation, growth and

differentiation9,13 (Fig. 2).

        WNT is the cornerstone of stem cell maintenance and proliferative expansion 14.

Canonical activation, through WNT3A-conditioned medium or the GSK3β inhibitor

CHIR99021, stabilizes β-catenin and induces the transcription of genes involved in cell-cycle

progression and stem cell identity14,48, but the level must be titrated: excessive activation

impairs differentiation and induces metaplasia, whereas insufficient signaling causes organoid

degeneration13,49. R-spondins act through LGR receptors to amplify this signal 50. In the gland,

LGR expression is cell-type-specific, with LGR4 predominant in ductal cells and LGR6

marking myoepithelial populations46,51, and lineage tracing has placed LGR5⁺ cells in a



                                                  7
progenitor pool shared between salivary gland and tongue epithelium 52. R-spondin 1 is

accordingly a standard medium component, synergizing with WNT activators to sustain robust

proliferation while preserving stem cell character14,53,54.

        The epidermal growth factor (EGF) superfamily supplies mitogenic and survival signals

through EGF receptor (EGFR) and related receptors55. EGF is the most common ligand and

supports proliferation, maturation and branching, though its concentration and identity bias

organoid morphology and lineage output13. The importance of signal intensity is well illustrated

in glandular systems: in human breastoid cultures, high-affinity EGF drives sustained ERK1/2–

RSK activation and an aberrant expansion of the myoepithelial lineage, whereas the lower-

affinity ligand amphiregulin, or a reduced EGF dose, restores normal ductal architecture 56.

Neuregulin-1 (NRG1), which signals preferentially through ErbB3/ErbB2 heterodimers and

the PI3K–AKT axis rather than the EGFR–MAPK pathway, has therefore been adopted to

replace EGF in long-term human salivary organoid culture while preserving glandular

identity13. The same NRG1/ErbB logic recurs across glandular organoid systems, supporting

long-term expansion in mammary cultures49 and NRG1/HER3-dependent antiandrogen

resistance in prostate organoid models57.

        Fibroblast growth factor (FGF) signaling, principally FGF10, governs branching. FGF

and WNT act as counteracting pathways, and their antagonism coordinates stalk elongation,

branching morphogenesis and end-bud maturation during gland development58. FGF10 acts as

a mesenchyme-derived signal through FGFR2b, engaging both ERK1/2 and AKT to regulate

branching and cell survival59,60; in culture, FGF10 supplementation enhances branching

complexity, though careful titration is needed to preserve organoid integrity61.

        Several inhibitory inputs must also be modulated. Noggin suppresses endogenous

bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) that would otherwise block organoid formation and drive

unwanted differentiation13,62 and the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) inhibitor A83-01



                                                 8
suppresses TGF-β–induced epithelial–mesenchymal transition in experimental cell systems 63,

and is used in salivary organoid culture13,89. However, TGF-β signaling has context-

dependent roles in salivary gland development and tissue remodeling 64, and its dysregulation

has been implicated in glandular fibrosis and dysfunction65.

       Notch is where niche control meets the field's central problem. Notch signaling supports

stem/progenitor maintenance and regenerative migration 66,6780, whereas differentiation media

containing the γ-secretase inhibitor DAPT can promote expression of acinar markers such as

AQP5 and MIST113. In human salivary organoids, DAPT was combined with withdrawal of

prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), nicotinamide and CHIR99021; therefore, the differentiation

response cannot be attributed to DAPT alone. Although differentiation competence was

retained during extended culture, the persistence of acinar identity after withdrawal of

differentiation cues and the effects of prolonged Notch suppression on ductal, basal and

myoepithelial populations remain insufficiently characterized. Moreover, DAPT inhibits γ-

secretase, which processes substrates beyond Notch, so additional substrate-dependent effects

cannot be excluded [NEW: Ran et al., 2017]. Hippo signaling, through YAP and TAZ,

integrates mechanical cues from the matrix to influence organoid size, shape and branching68,69.



Culture Systems and Technical Considerations

Culture methods have advanced from simple suspensions to defined systems, and the physical

and chemical environment shapes development at every step9,21. Extracellular matrix (ECM)

choice is the most consequential decision. Matrigel, a laminin- and collagen-IV-rich basement-

membrane extract, remains the standard and supplies the cues for three-dimensional

organization and branching61,70, but its undefined composition, batch variability and xenogeneic

origin obstruct standardization and clinical translation 71. The demonstration that a chemically

defined, Matrigel-free system yields functional human organoids with improved epithelial



                                               9
polarity and acinar maturation is therefore significant on two counts: improved translational

compatibility and enhanced acinar competence21. Defined alternatives are expanding:

poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) hydrogels functionalized with adhesion peptides 72,73,

decellularized salivary ECM that retains tissue-specific cues74, and alginate–gelatin composites

that support acinar expansion at low cost with easy cell recovery75. Experimental studies further

illustrate the importance of matrix properties: enzymatically degradable PEG hydrogels

promote acinar-associated characteristics in primary salivary gland cells⁷³, while reversible

thermo-ionically crosslinked hydrogels support the expansion of functional human salivary

acinar spheroids⁷⁵. Together, these findings suggest that matrix composition, degradability and

mechanical context influence epithelial phenotypes, although the independent contributions of

these factors remain incompletely resolved.

        Medium      composition   balances    growth    against   differentiation.   Adult-tissue

formulations combine EGF, R-spondin 1 and a WNT activator with FGF10, Noggin, a TGF-β

inhibitor and the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 13,14,62,76. Beyond composition, the timing of factor

addition and withdrawal is decisive, particularly for PSC-derived organoids that require

sequential pathway activation to specify lineage and mature47.

        Passaging and storage introduce additional sources of variability. Organoids are

typically passaged mechanically or enzymatically at protocol-dependent intervals 11,13,77, and

dissociation can disrupt architecture and alter the representation of epithelial subtypes 23.

Cryopreserved cultures therefore require post-thaw assessment of viability and epithelial

composition23. Because acinar populations can be clonally heterogeneous in another exocrine

organ, the pancreas78, longitudinal monitoring of cellular composition across the culture

lifecycle is essential.

        As a two-dimensional alternative, chemical reprogramming culture uses a feeder- and

Matrigel-free defined medium with small-molecule inhibitors to expand human salivary basal



                                               10
progenitors while preserving ductal progenitor identity and multilineage potential 79. By

supplying defined progenitors under simple, xeno-reduced conditions, it offers a cell source

that feeds directly into cell-based therapy, a strategy we return to in the section on regeneration.



CHARACTERIZATION AND FUNCTIONAL VALIDATION

Structural Analysis and Molecular Markers

Validating salivary gland organoids begins with their structural organization and molecular

composition14,23,77,80. Characterization draws on immunofluorescence, histological staining,

bulk and single-cell expression profiling, ultrastructural analysis, and functional readouts such

as mucin/glycoprotein staining, amylase activity and calcium mobilization14,61,77.

       The native epithelium is compartmentalized into luminal ductal and acinar cells and

abluminal basal and myoepithelial cells, so well-differentiated organoids should be assessed

for lumen formation, polarized ductal markers, acinar/secretory induction and abluminal

features14,61,77. Acinar cells are marked by AQP5, BHLHA15 and AMY, ductal cells by KRT7,

KRT8/18 and KRT19, basal cells by KRT5, KRT14 and TP63, and myoepithelial cells by co-

expression of KRT14 and ACTA214,36,61,77,79. AQP5 localization is especially informative: apical

enrichment in acinar-like cells signals the epithelial polarization required for vectorial water

movement and fluid secretion81,82.

       A range of markers, including ITGB1/CD29, CD44, CD24, CD49f, EpCAM, SOX9,

SOX10, KRT5/KRT14, TP63 and context-dependent KIT/CD117, has been linked to

stem/progenitor-like states, but these are best read as context-dependent indicators of

regenerative potential and lineage bias rather than markers of any universal salivary gland stem

cell23,41,80,83-85. Their spatial localization is nonetheless informative: in intestinal and gastric

organoids, marker mapping has resolved stem cell niches and guided reverse identification of

native progenitor populations29,86,87, an approach that may help settle debate over salivary



                                                11
stem/progenitor identity23,80,88. The transcription factors SOX2, SOX9, and SOX10 mark

developmental, progenitor, or secretory-lineage programs relevant to self-renewal and

differentiation capacity in salivary gland models41,42,85. Single-cell transcriptomics reinforces

this, showing model- and stage-dependent mixtures of ductal, basal, progenitor-like, pro-acinar

and myoepithelial-like cells rather than a single hierarchy 36,77,80. Histology and ultrastructure

add the spatial view, with periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) and Alcian blue staining marking mucin-

producing cells and electron microscopy resolving serous and mucous acinar-like cells with

electron-dense or electron-pale vesicles alongside myoepithelial cells14,77.



Functional Assays

Functional validation extends beyond structure to the processes that define glandular function:

secretory capacity, ion transport and neurotransmitter responsiveness14,89 (Fig. 3).

        Calcium assays probe the signaling underlying secretion using fluorescent indicators

such as Fluo-4 to track cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ after secretagogue stimulation89,90. In salivary organoids,

carbachol and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) elicit calcium transients that indicate intact secretory

signaling13.

        Swelling assays offer a visually intuitive readout of fluid secretion. Differentiated

salivary organoids can swell in response to carbachol, isoproterenol and vasoactive intestinal

peptide13, while carbachol- and forskolin-induced swelling has also been demonstrated in

human salivary gland-derived organoids89. Forskolin-induced swelling was first established in

intestinal organoids as a cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR)-

dependent measure of epithelial anion and fluid transport91. The magnitude and kinetics of

swelling, tracked by time-lapse imaging, report integrated ion-channel, transporter and water-

channel activity.

        Amylase production is a defining acinar readout because the enzyme is a major




                                                12
salivary component14. It can be assessed by expression, intracellular localization or enzyme-

activity measurements and interpreted alongside other secretory markers 13,14,90. Chloride-

transport function can be assessed using complementary pathway-specific readouts. CFTR-dependent

forskolin swelling 91 and ANO1/TMEM16A-dependent salivary secretion92 provide relevant pathway

benchmarks. Halide-sensitive fluorescent indicators, when combined with appropriate stimulation or

inhibition, may provide a complementary readout of chloride flux, although direct validation of such

assays in salivary organoid systems remains limited. Ussing-chamber recordings on organoid-derived

monolayers can provide more detailed electrophysiological measurements when technically feasible.



Species Differences: Murine versus Human Organoid Models

This divergence is the developmental tension in concrete form: the degree of acinar maturation

a system achieves sets the ceiling on the secretory, pharmacological and regenerative functions

it can later support. The gap between murine and human systems is the field's defining

methodological challenge, with direct consequences for translational research 13,93-95 (Fig. 4). It

reflects not only species-specific gland anatomy and secretory specialization but also divergent

responses to niche factors and extracellular matrix, together with the effects of donor age,

pathology, and culture-induced selection.

       Murine salivary gland organoids can exhibit acinar, ductal and myoepithelial lineage

features under optimized differentiation conditions, although the extent of differentiation varies

with gland of origin and culture protocol13,14,61. They form lumenized or branched structures

expressing gland-associated secretory markers such as AQP5, BHLHA15, AMY and mucin-

related proteins, depending on the gland of origin and protocol13,14,61. Differentiation outcomes

may also vary with passage history and matrix conditions, with batch-variable matrices

introducing an additional source of experimental variability⁷¹. Ultrastructural analyses confirm

secretory maturation, revealing differentiated serous and mucous acinar-like cells with

secretory vesicles and associated myoepithelial cells 14, while functional assays demonstrate


                                                13
secretagogue-responsive activity, including neurotransmitter-induced swelling, amylase

secretion and calcium mobilization13,14,96.

        Human organoids, by contrast, are typically ductal- or basal/progenitor-enriched

under standard expansion, forming cystic, solid, dense or branched structures with basal cells

expressing KRT5 and KRT14 and luminal cells expressing KRT7 and KRT19; robust

expression of acinar markers such as AQP5, BHLHA15, AMY1A and lactoferrin remains

protocol-dependent13,77,79,89. Although WNT/EGF-rich expansion conditions may favor

proliferative ductal and basal states, their contribution to limited acinar maturation relative to

starting cell composition, gland of origin, passage-dependent selection, ECM composition and

other niche cues remains incompletely resolved13,14,77.13,21,23,77

        Several strategies have been used to address this limitation. Targeted differentiation

approaches, including Notch inhibition with DAPT, reduction of expansion-associated

supplements and modulation of ALK/TGF-β signaling, have promoted acinar or secretory

marker expression and functional responses13,77,89, while matrix-based approaches such as

collagen-based organoids, hyaluronate hydrogels and salivary gland-derived decellularized

ECM aim to improve epithelial organization and clinical applicability 77,97,98. Chemically

defined, xeno-free or Matrigel-free systems have gone furthest, achieving multilineage

differentiation with preserved epithelial heterogeneity and a single-cell basal-to-ductal-to-

acinar trajectory21, yet complete acinar maturation and full recapitulation of native secretory

output remain unresolved21,77.

        These differences are likely multifactorial, but the relative contributions of intrinsic

species biology and culture-dependent factors remain unresolved. Potential contributors

include species-specific gland architecture and transcriptomic programs, stem/progenitor

states, divergent WNT, EGF, FGF, Notch and TGF-β/ALK requirements, matrix-dependent

responses, and donor variables such as age, disease, inflammation and prior injury 13,23,93-95,99.



                                                  14
Many human cultures originate from adult, donor-variable, ductal/basal-enriched populations

and may require additional niche cues for acinar maturation; however, determining whether

these factors account for the observed species differences will require comparisons under more

closely matched culture and differentiation conditions. The widespread use of mouse-sarcoma-

derived Matrigel further introduces undefined, batch-variable ECM, although direct evidence

that murine matrix alone impairs human acinar maturation remains limited77,98.

       These properties define complementary roles. Murine organoids, experimentally

tractable and readily differentiated, are well suited to studying gland morphogenesis, lineage

specification, secretagogue responses and injury–repair mechanisms 13,14,61; human organoids,

despite their ductal/progenitor bias, are indispensable for translation because they preserve

patient-specific genetic backgrounds for modeling disease susceptibility, radiation injury,

tumor biology and personalized therapeutic responses13,23,77. The two systems are most powerful

when used together (Table 2). For secretory pharmacology and regenerative therapy, the

maturity of the epithelial compartment also determines how closely experimental readouts

approximate native gland function.



DISEASE MODELING I: TUMOR ORGANOIDS AND PRECISION ONCOLOGY

The Unique Landscape of Salivary Gland Tumor Diversity

Salivary gland cancers comprise 21 malignant epithelial tumor types in the fifth edition of the

WHO Classification of Head and Neck Tumours, each with distinct histopathology, molecular

profile, biological behavior and clinical outcome7,100. The classification increasingly

incorporates recurrent molecular alterations, particularly tumor-type-specific gene fusions, into

diagnostic definitions and, in selected subtypes, therapeutic decisions 7. This heterogeneity

translates into wide variation in disease course and treatment response, which frustrates broadly

applicable strategies and makes the case for subtype-specific precision oncology. Because most



                                               15
subtypes are rare, biomarker studies and subtype-specific clinical trials are frequently

underpowered17,101-103. Modeling this diversity brings the architectural tension to the fore:

faithful patient-derived organoids must preserve subtype identity while resolving neoplastic

epithelium from the stromal and immune compartments that current cultures largely omit.

         The spectrum runs from the more common mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) and

adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) to rare variants such as epithelial–myoepithelial carcinoma

(EMC),       carcinoma          ex   pleomorphic   adenoma   (CaExPA)   and   polymorphous

adenocarcinoma7,100. Each carries characteristic morphology and molecular alterations that

reflect distinct cellular origins and pathogenetic mechanisms. MEC frequently harbors

CRTC1/3–MAML2 rearrangements that activate CREB-associated transcriptional programs

and AREG–EGFR signaling104-106; ACC shows cribriform, tubular and solid growth patterns

with MYB–NFIB or MYBL1–NFIB fusions107-109, alongside recurrent alterations in chromatin-

remodeling complexes, DNA-damage-response pathways and NOTCH signaling 110-112 (Fig. 5

and Table 3).



Patient-Derived Organoids: Establishment and Characterization

Patient-derived organoids (PDOs) model individual salivary gland tumor subtypes while

preserving the key histopathological, molecular and differentiation features of the parental

tumor, and collections spanning multiple subtypes are beginning to provide experimentally

tractable models of a disease spectrum that has been poorly represented in conventional cell

culture systems16,17,101,113.

        Establishment typically begins with fresh surgical specimens, followed by controlled

mechanical and enzymatic dissociation designed to preserve viable tumor epithelial clusters

while minimizing single-cell stress and stromal contamination 114,115. Reported success rates

vary substantially and depend on how establishment is defined, including short-term organoid



                                                   16
formation, serial passaging capacity and recovery after cryopreservation. In one systematic

effort, viable short-term cultures were generated from 7 of 37 salivary gland cancer

specimens16. Such selective establishment may favor tumors that grow readily in culture while

underrepresenting other subtypes or cell populations, potentially limiting the generalizability

of PDO-based findings. Contributing factors include specimen size and cellularity, necrotic

burden, ischemic and processing time, intrinsic growth kinetics, prior treatment exposure and

the proportion of viable tumor epithelium116,117.

        A particularly important challenge is the admixture or overgrowth of residual normal

salivary epithelial, basal/myoepithelial or stromal populations during early passage 118,119, an

issue that is especially complex in biphasic tumors, where neoplastic luminal and abluminal

populations must be distinguished from residual normal ductal, basal and myoepithelial

cells120,121. Rigorous characterization should therefore combine histomorphological comparison

with the parental tumor, immunophenotypic assessment of lineage-specific markers, and

molecular authentication using fusion testing, targeted sequencing, copy-number profiling,

whole-exome sequencing or RNA sequencing16,101,113(Box 1->Table 4). Because serial passaging

can drive clonal selection, loss of minor cell populations and transcriptomic drift, particularly

in slow-growing tumors such as ACC, passage number should be documented and key

histological and molecular features reassessed at defined intervals, with retention of defining

alterations such as MYB–NFIB regarded as a key criterion of disease relevance16,101. Although

long-term establishment remains inconsistent, PDOs have been reported from multiple

subtypes, including ACC, MEC, salivary duct carcinoma (SDC), myoepithelial carcinoma and

secretory carcinoma, demonstrating feasibility while underscoring the need for subtype-

specific culture conditions and standardized authentication criteria16,101,102,113.



Precision Medicine: Drug Screening and Therapeutic Stratification



                                                  17
For localized disease, surgery with risk-adapted radiotherapy remains the treatment backbone;

for recurrent or metastatic disease, however, systemic options remain limited and increasingly

depend on molecular profiling and subtype-specific targets. In this context, PDO-based drug

screening adds a functional validation layer to genomic precision oncology by testing whether

molecular alterations translate into actual drug sensitivity in patient-derived tumor cells113,122,123.

This is particularly valuable for salivary gland cancers, where treatment options are limited and

individual subtypes are too rare for conventionally powered trials124,125.

        Organoid-based platforms evaluate multiple compounds and dose ranges in parallel,

with screening capacity determined by organoid expansion and assay format, and ATP-based

luminescence assays or metabolic dyes quantify organoid viability after treatment 123,126. Drug

concentrations and exposure schedules should be selected with reference to clinically

achievable pharmacokinetics rather than arbitrary in vitro dose ranges 126,127. Building on

genomic diagnosis, organoid-based functional testing can confirm, refine or challenge

predicted therapeutic vulnerabilities, as demonstrated by tropomyosin receptor kinase (TRK)

inhibitor testing in ETV6–NTRK3 fusion-positive secretory carcinoma 102 and by genotype-

oriented pathway testing in salivary gland cancer organoids 113. For human epidermal growth

factor receptor 2 (HER2)-amplified or androgen receptor (AR)-positive SDC, PDOs may

support patient-specific testing of HER2-directed agents, androgen receptor inhibition and

selected combination regimens. This functional assessment helps determine whether a

molecularly or immunophenotypically defined target represents a true therapeutic dependency

rather than merely a detectable biomarker 113,125,128. The applications of PDOs also differ by

tumor subtype. In SDC, organoids have supported genotype-informed testing of AR

antagonism and PI3K–mTOR/RAS–MAPK pathway inhibitors, although incomplete retention

of HER2 amplification in some cultures highlights the need for molecular reauthentication¹¹³.

In CRTC1/3–MAML2-positive MEC, AREG–EGFR signaling provides a candidate target for



                                                  18
PDO-based screening, based on findings from cell-line and xenograft studies rather than

validated MEC PDO drug responses¹⁰⁴–¹⁰⁶. ACC organoids may facilitate studies of

MYB/MYBL1 fusion-associated biology and molecularly selected NOTCH-pathway

vulnerabilities, although slow growth and culture-associated selection can complicate

longitudinal studies and drug screening¹⁰¹,¹⁰⁷–¹¹³. For rarer subtypes, establishing representative

PDO models and verifying tumor identity using subtype-appropriate authentication criteria

(Table 4) remain prerequisites for meaningful therapeutic evaluation¹⁰¹,¹¹³.

        Beyond single agents, organoids provide a preclinical platform for prioritizing

rational drug combinations before clinical evaluation, an especially useful function in rare

cancers for which large combination trials are difficult to conduct113,123. Although salivary gland

cancer-specific radiosensitivity data remain limited, organoids from other head and neck

cancers provide proof of principle that patient-derived models can reproduce interpatient

variation in radiation response129,130. Across sufficiently large PDO cohorts, integrating drug-

response profiles with genomic and transcriptomic data may identify predictive biomarkers and

define molecularly informed responder subgroups, which can then be evaluated in registries,

basket trials or prospective clinical studies17,113.

        Clinical implementation still faces practical challenges, including establishment

timelines compatible with treatment decision-making, standardized culture and assay

protocols, tumor-cell authentication, sufficient assay success rates and prospective clinical

validation, together with the selection bias introduced when particular subtypes or rapidly

growing clones establish preferentially123,127,131. When establishment and drug testing take

weeks to months, PDO testing may be more feasible for informing subsequent treatment lines

than urgent initial decisions in rapidly progressing disease. Prospective studies should report

the proportion of patients receiving interpretable results within the relevant treatment decision

window123,127,131. A registered prospective, multicenter observational study (NCT06686979;



                                                  19
planned enrollment, n = 40) is evaluating concordance between PDO drug sensitivity and

clinical treatment outcomes in recurrent or metastatic salivary gland cancer. Patients receive

guideline- or physician-directed systemic therapy independently of PDO testing, while biopsy-

derived organoids undergo parallel drug-sensitivity assessment. The registered primary

outcomes are objective response rate and disease control rate over two years, with secondary

outcomes including PDO establishment and testing success, ex vivo tumor growth inhibition,

progression-free survival and overall survival. Although designed as a multicenter study, the

public registry currently identifies Huashan Hospital as the only named study institution,

leaving the number of distinct participating centers unclear [N1]. With prospective validation

and protocol standardization, PDO-based functional testing may complement genomic,

pathological and clinical information to support more rational therapeutic selection for patients

with limited treatment options. Accordingly, translational relevance depends on both

preserving disease-relevant tumor features and obtaining interpretable results within the

treatment decision window, while incorporating stromal and immune influences remains a

further architectural challenge.



DISEASE       MODELING             II:   SJÖGREN'S       DISEASE       AND      AUTOIMMUNE

ORGANOIDS

Sjögren’s disease (SjD) is a prototypical autoimmune condition in which progressive

dysfunction and destruction of the salivary and lacrimal glands cause debilitating sicca

symptoms and, in a subset of patients, systemic manifestations132-134. It is the clearest expression

of the architectural tension: because the disease is driven by immune–epithelial and stromal–

epithelial crosstalk, epithelial-only organoids capture only part of its biology, and

reconstituting these interactions could extend disease modeling beyond the epithelial-intrinsic

processes captured by current organoids20,135,136



                                                20
Epithelial-Intrinsic Dysfunction in Sjögren's Disease

Organoids derived from SjD patient biopsies reproduce disease-relevant epithelial features

even without immune cells, including persistent interferon and Janus kinase–signal transducer

and activator of transcription (JAK–STAT) signaling, altered epithelial phenotypes and

impaired functional responses20,137. Long-term organoids from healthy and SjD human minor

(labial) glands retain native gland-specific phenotype and secretory function, and single-cell

transcriptomics resolves distinct phenotypic differences between healthy- and patient-derived

organoids, indicating that disease-associated epithelial programs persist in culture 137. Tight-

junction disruption, a recognized abnormality of SjD epithelium linked to inflammatory

cytokine exposure, has yet to be directly demonstrated in patient-derived organoids 132,138.

Because these intrinsic alterations are quantifiable in vitro, epithelial-only organoids provide a

controlled system to quantify disease-associated epithelial phenotypes and therapeutic

responses and to define the epithelium's autonomous contribution to pathogenesis, independent

of ongoing immune attack20,136,137.



Chimeric Organoid Models: Stromal-Epithelial and Immune-Epithelial Crosstalk

Direct studies of human SjD-derived salivary gland organoids have primarily characterized

epithelial-intrinsic dysfunction²⁰,¹³⁷, whereas immune and stromal cell populations have been

characterized in native SjD gland tissue¹³⁹. Multimodal profiling of human SjD glands specifies

which partners to reconstitute: single-cell and spatial analyses identify cytotoxic

GZMB⁺GNLY⁺ CD8⁺ T cells as the dominant expanded effector across autoantibody subgroups

and nominate complement- and chemokine-expressing THY1⁺ fibroblasts as stromal hubs of

inflammation139, thereby defining the immune and stromal components for causal testing in

chimeric organoids.



                                               21
         Because epithelial-only cultures cannot capture the interactions that sustain

autoimmune injury, recent work has moved toward immune-integrated systems, with proof of

principle emerging largely from other tissues. In the intestine, air–liquid-interface organoids

grown directly from endoscopic biopsies preserve epithelium, native mesenchyme and tissue-

resident immune cells as a single unit and reproduce antigen-driven, immune-mediated

epithelial destruction, establishing a general framework for organoid modeling of

autoimmunity140. A complementary strategy integrates autologous tissue-resident memory T

cells into epithelial organoids, where they engraft and, upon activation, drive cytotoxic injury

that can be pharmacologically modulated141. Applied to the salivary gland, analogous co-culture

and chimeric strategies that combine organoids with selected immune populations,

inflammatory cytokines or patient-derived immune components could model autoantigen

presentation, interferon responses and functional decline that are difficult to isolate in

vivo132,135.

  Inflammatory epithelial–stromal interactions have been implicated in salivary gland

dysfunction in SjD, providing a rationale for investigating whether pathways such as tumor

necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)/nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) influence acinar differentiation 132,135.

Recent work using murine chimeric salivary gland organoids provides direct experimental

evidence that multicellular stromal environments can be incorporated into salivary organoid

models. In this system, embryonic submandibular stromal cells were combined with postnatal

epithelial organoids, introducing fibroblast, macrophage/monocyte-like, endothelial and

neuronal populations [NEW: Yoon et al., JDR, in press]. Single-cell analyses and stromal

fractionation identified PTPRC-enriched stromal cells as a candidate source of TNF-

superfamily ligand transcripts, while TNF-α exposure reduced selected acinar-associated

markers and dexamethasone or NF-κB pathway inhibition increased their expression under the

tested conditions [NEW: Yoon et al., JDR, in press]. These findings provide a tractable model



                                              22
for investigating inflammation-associated stromal–epithelial interactions but do not establish

direct cell-specific causality or fully restored acinar maturation. Further validation in patient-

derived human SjD organoids is needed to determine whether comparable inflammatory

interactions contribute to impaired regeneration and loss of secretory identity.



Precision Immunology: Patient-Specific Therapeutic Modeling and Biomarker Discovery

Next-generation immuno-organoid and assembloid systems aim to reconstruct autoimmune

injury with greater cellular and spatial complexity by incorporating autologous, tissue-resident

immune compartments while preserving endogenous immune diversity 142. If developed for

salivary gland disease, assembloids combining epithelial organoids with autologous immune

cells and stromal fibroblasts could enable dynamic study of immune–epithelial crosstalk and,

crucially, patient-specific testing of immunomodulatory drugs135,143. No mature human salivary

gland immune–epithelial assembloid has yet been reported; proposed immune-integrated

models for SjD therefore draw on approaches developed in other tissues¹⁴ ⁰,¹⁴¹. The ability of

patient-derived SjD organoids to report pharmacological responses has already been

demonstrated using the JAK inhibitor tofacitinib, which enhanced their proliferation by

suppressing JAK–STAT signaling137, providing a concrete example of organoid-based

therapeutic evaluation. Such models could also help dissect disease mechanisms and support

therapeutic target nomination and validation141.

       These considerations matter given the clinical heterogeneity of SjD. Patient glands

stratified by autoantibody status carry distinct signatures characterized by an activated

interferon program in anti-SSA-positive disease and enhanced TGF-β signaling in anti-

centromere-positive disease, implying different actionable pathways among serologic

subgroups139. Reconstructing these subgroup-specific signatures in patient-derived organoids

and linking clinical clustering to functional readouts could support patient-specific prediction



                                               23
of therapeutic responses and biomarker discovery, thereby extending the precision oncology

paradigm into a precision immunology framework for autoimmune salivary gland disease. The

architectural challenge is therefore to reconstruct disease-relevant immune–stromal

interactions without losing the experimental resolution afforded by epithelial-only organoids.



DISEASE MODELING III: RADIATION-INDUCED SALIVARY DYSFUNCTION

Radiation-induced salivary dysfunction is a frequent and debilitating consequence of head and

neck cancer treatment. Most patients receiving radiotherapy develop some xerostomia, though

estimates of clinically significant hyposalivation vary with dose, irradiated gland volume,

treatment modality, salivary endpoint and follow-up, and the resulting chronic oral

complications severely affect quality of life144,145. Organoid models and salivary tissue-chip

platforms capture selected acute and chronic features of radiation injury, supporting

mechanistic study, radioprotective screening and regenerative therapy testing77,146-148.



Acute Radiation Injury: DNA Damage, Apoptosis, and Secretory Dysfunction

During the acute phase, within the first week after irradiation, mouse and human salivary

organoid-derived cells have been used to assess DNA-damage responses and organoid-forming

survival148, whereas murine salivary tissue chips have reproduced dose-dependent γH2AX and

53BP1 responses benchmarked against irradiated mouse gland tissue 147. Other mechanisms

implicated in salivary radiation injury include reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation,

mitochondrial dysfunction, aberrant calcium signaling, p53-dependent apoptosis, epithelial

tight-junction disruption and altered localization or expression of the water channel AQP5144;

these features have not all been demonstrated in each model. Double-strand-break markers

such as γ-H2AX, together with calcium and secretory assays, provide complementary rather

than interchangeable measures of early injury147,148.



                                               24
Chronic Sequelae: Cellular Senescence and Niche Impairment

Chronic-phase studies in mouse and human salivary glands and murine organoids capture long-

term sequelae: cellular senescence marked by p16INK4a upregulation, persistent DNA-damage

signaling and a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), alongside progressive

impairment of the stem/progenitor niche146,149. SASP factors, including pro-inflammatory

cytokines (IL-6, IL-8), matrix metalloproteinases and extracellular vesicles, may act non-cell-

autonomously, propagating senescence through paracrine signaling and TGF-β-associated

stromal fibrosis; epithelial-only organoids cannot reproduce the full stromal response.

Although many experimental studies use a single acute high dose, fractionation can produce

endpoint-dependent effects: four daily fractions of 0.25 Gy reduced organoid-forming survival

more than a single 1 Gy dose in mouse salivary organoid-derived cells148, whereas single doses

of 10 or 15 Gy and three fractions of 5 Gy produced broadly comparable acute injury at day 3

in an in vivo mouse study [NEW: Johnson et al., 2024]. These results do not establish

equivalence in long-term gland function or clinical radiotherapy. Spatial dose distribution is

also important, because sparing stem-cell-rich gland regions preserves salivary output150.

However, conventional organoids do not recapitulate intact-gland anatomy or the spatial dose

gradients needed to assess region-specific sparing.



Organoid Platforms for Radioprotective and Senolytic Drug Screening

Murine salivary tissue chips have been used to assess WR-1065 radioprotection, with DNA-

damage responses benchmarked against irradiated mouse gland tissue 147. A subsequent high-

content tissue-chip screen evaluated 438 approved compounds, identified 25 double hits and

tested three candidates in irradiated mice [NEW: Piraino et al., 2025]. Separately, senescence-

targeted treatment improved organoid-forming efficiency in irradiated murine salivary



                                              25
organoids, while ABT263 preserved salivary function in irradiated mice 146. These studies

demonstrate screening feasibility but highlight the need to establish assay robustness,

throughput, reproducibility across preparations and batches, and dose-response performance

against appropriate controls. DNA-damage and senescence-marker responses should be

assessed alongside functional endpoints, including organoid formation and salivary flow;

reduced p16, p21 or SASP-marker expression alone does not demonstrate selective senescent-

cell clearance. Because conventional epithelial organoids lack stromal and immune

compartments, multicellular co-culture or tissue-chip systems are needed to model this

complexity and its pharmacological modulation. In irradiated mouse recipients, human salivary

organoid xenografts demonstrated engraftment, whereas murine organoid allografts increased

salivary secretion77. These preclinical findings support regenerative potential, although

engraftment durability, long-term safety and scalability remain unresolved. Coupled with

salivary-specific assays of amylase secretion, calcium transients and tight-junction integrity,

these platforms bridge phenotypic screening and functional pharmacology.



FUNCTIONAL PHARMACOLOGY PLATFORMS

Building on their use in radioprotective and senolytic screening, salivary gland organoids are

emerging as functional pharmacology platforms for agents acting on epithelial secretion, tissue

regeneration and neurosecretory signaling. This shifts the organoid from a disease model to a

quantitative assay of agonist responsiveness, secretory competence, drug potency and

pharmacodynamic effect, although regulatory or industrial use will require assay qualification,

benchmarking against accepted reference methods and cross-laboratory reproducibility.



Translational Pharmacology for Xerostomia Therapeutics

One potential application is the preclinical evaluation of agents that modulate salivary



                                              26
neurosecretory function. Cholinergic agonists such as pilocarpine and cevimeline, established

treatments for xerostomia and salivary hypofunction, may be assessed using organoid secretory

readouts to examine receptor responsiveness, residual secretory capacity and concentration-

dependent effects13,145,151. Pilocarpine-induced swelling has been demonstrated in organoids

derived from human labial salivary gland biopsies²⁰, whereas comparable organoid-based

evaluation of cevimeline remains to be established. These assays provide functional

pharmacodynamic readouts rather than clinical dose optimization, since systemic tolerability,

autonomic side effects, pharmacokinetics and residual gland volume still govern patient

response.

        Novel secretagogues in development, including muscarinic-targeted agents, purinergic

modulators, ion-channel regulators and compounds restoring calcium-dependent secretion, need

platforms that recapitulate integrated epithelial secretion13,151,152. Because salivary organoids

report calcium signaling, swelling and gland-type-specific secretory markers, they serve as a

practical intermediate between reductionist receptor assays and in vivo salivation studies13,89. This

intermediate role is clinically motivated: cholinergic secretagogues act only where residual

functional acinar tissue remains and so offer limited benefit after extensive radiation- or

autoimmune-mediated acinar loss. Organoid platforms that couple secretagogue testing with

regenerative readouts could therefore help identify agents or agent–cell combinations that restore

secretion in glands with depleted acinar reserves, a population poorly served by current

pharmacotherapy.



Botulinum Neurotoxin Potency Testing

A prominent neuroglandular application is botulinum neurotoxin (BoNT) potency testing, which

traditionally relies on the mouse median lethal dose (LD50) assay, an approach that raises ethical

concerns, consumes large animal numbers and is subject to variability in injection, endpoint and



                                                 27
interlaboratory implementation that complicates standardization153-155. Validated cell-based

potency assays (CBPAs), particularly those measuring BoNT uptake and cleavage of

synaptosomal-associated protein 25 (SNAP25) in neuronal systems, have achieved regulatory

acceptance for selected BoNT/A products156-159. But these model neuronal intoxication rather than

gland-specific neuroepithelial responses, so salivary organoids are best positioned as

complementary gland-specific pharmacodynamic platforms rather than replacements.

       This application has direct clinical grounding. Beyond its neuromuscular indications,

botulinum neurotoxin is injected into the parotid and submandibular glands to treat sialorrhea in

Parkinson's disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, cerebral palsy and other neurological

conditions, where the therapeutic aim is to reduce glandular secretory output rather than to block

skeletal muscle160,161. Mechanistically, BoNT inhibits acetylcholine release at cholinergic

terminals of the neuroglandular junction, so an assay that reads the glandular secretory response

measures the pharmacodynamic endpoint most relevant to this indication. In murine salivary

gland organoid (SGO)–Neuro-2a (N2a) neuronal co-cultures, neuronal processes contact the

organoid surface, acetylcholine or neostigmine stimulation increases the expression of

secretory markers such as Aqp5 and Bhlha15, and BoNT/A treatment reverses these responses,

supporting a neuroepithelial organoid-based BoNT assay162. Because neuronal cell-based

potency assays were developed and optimized for neuromuscular products, they do not directly

capture this gland-specific pharmacology; the next step is therefore to benchmark these readouts

against established potency standards, test whether they add predictive value, and demonstrate

reproducibility across laboratories and BoNT serotypes.



Organoid Platform for Fibrosis and Inflammation

Comprehensive     pharmacology      must   also     address   the   fibrotic   and   inflammatory

microenvironments driving chronic dysfunction in radiation injury and Sjögren’s disease.



                                               28
Radiation-induced senescence and cytokine signaling can impair the regenerative niche149,

providing a rationale for testing cell-free therapeutics, particularly extracellular vesicles (EVs).

Evidence from intestinal163, hepatic164 and retinal165 models supports the broader anti-

inflammatory and antifibrotic potential of EVs but does not constitute direct salivary gland

evidence. In the salivary gland, CD9-enriched EVs from chemically reprogrammed basal

progenitors attenuated fibrosis in human salivary organoid assays and a mouse duct-obstruct

model166. In organoid-based assays, EVs could be evaluated for their ability to reduce

inflammatory or fibrotic signaling and restore epithelial progenitor function. However,

assessment of stromal fibrosis or immune modulation requires the relevant non-

epithelial compartments. A remaining limitation is scalability: multicellular organoids carry

inherent complexity and batch variability, and nonstandardized ECM and inconsistent immune-

cell ratios constrain high-throughput use167,168.



Regulatory Implications and the 3R Framework

These advances answer the growing demand for animal-free or animal-reducing methods and

align with the Replacement, Reduction and Refinement (3R) principles increasingly emphasized

by regulators and pharmacopeial bodies153. The European Pharmacopoeia already permits BoNT

potency testing by LD50 or by methods validated against it, and product-specific CBPAs show

that non-animal testing can meet regulatory expectations for selected BoNT products 156,158,159.

Salivary organoid–neuronal co-culture assays may provide complementary readouts of gland-

specific pharmacodynamics, but, unlike validated product-specific neuronal CBPAs, they

remain exploratory and are not accepted as regulatory lot-release potency assays for BoNT.

Their further development requires standardized co-culture conditions, benchmarking against

established potency methods and demonstrated cross-laboratory reproducibility; organoid-

based evaluation of complex biologics such as EVs also remains investigational.

REGENERATIVE THERAPEUTICS

Organoid Transplantation: From Preclinical Validation to Clinical Trials

Regeneration is where the three tensions converge on the clinic: a transplantable product must

achieve acinar maturation (developmental), integrate with stromal, vascular and neural

compartments (architectural), and be manufactured reproducibly under regulatory constraints

(translational). Transplantation illustrates this convergence most directly, and has progressed

from proof-of-concept studies to early clinical evaluation, representing one of the most advanced

directions in the field15,21,22,169. Preclinical work from several groups has established the feasibility

of organoid or stem/progenitor cell transplantation to restore gland function. Human salivary

stem/progenitor cells grown as salispheres or organoids self-renew, differentiate into

multilineage organoids, engraft in irradiated mouse glands and partially restore saliva production

in xenotransplantation models15. Orthotopic transplantation of human organoids into the

submandibular glands of non-obese diabetic (NOD) mice, a Sjögren's disease model,

significantly improved stimulated salivary flow, increased acinar AQP5 expression and

promoted structural integration within the host ductal network21. Lymphocytic focus scores were

unchanged, suggesting that improvement was driven by epithelial restoration rather than broad

immunosuppression21; green fluorescent protein (GFP) tracing confirmed survival and ductal

integration, and proteomic analysis of saliva detected human-specific salivary proteins, providing

direct evidence of secretory activity from engrafted cells21. Ectopic transplantation under the

renal capsule further showed that human organoids can undergo multilineage differentiation and

form gland-like structures with mucous-rich lumens, secretory features and vascular integration

even outside the native niche, and co-transplantation with embryonic murine salivary

mesenchyme enhanced AQP5- and MUC7-positive acinar-like differentiation, consistent with

mesenchyme-associated acinar maturation in this ectopic mouse model21. Even so, these



                                                   30
observations do not establish mature neural or immune integration, nor physiological drainage

into the oral cavity.

  Comparable feasibility holds across matrices and species. In a recent study, human salivary

stem cells expanded as Matrigel- or collagen-based human salivary gland organoids (hSGOs) and

differentiated into acinar and ductal lineages; collagen-based hSGOs engrafted in mouse

recipients and expressed mucins such as MUC5B, while allogeneic transplantation of murine

SGOs increased salivation in a radiation-induced xerostomia model77. As a foundational

resource, a clinically annotated salivary tissue-organoid biorepository from 208 donors has been

established at Mayo Clinic, supporting stem/progenitor characterization, regenerative assay

development and future translational studies23 (Table 4).

  Clinical translation has advanced most clearly in the RESTART program at the University

Medical Center Groningen, where autologous organoid-derived cells are being evaluated to

prevent or treat radiation-induced xerostomia after head and neck cancer therapy22,169. RESTART

(NCT04593589) is an open-label, single-arm phase I trial at one center, with a planned

enrollment of 18 adults undergoing surgery for oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma followed by

postoperative (chemo)radiotherapy involving the remaining submandibular gland. Patients with

systemic diseases affecting salivary function, including Sjögren’s disease, are excluded.

  Salivary tissue is obtained before radiotherapy, stem/progenitor cells are expanded ex vivo as

organoid-derived cell products under good manufacturing practice (GMP)-compatible

conditions, and the product is reintroduced after treatment 22. Public participant information

issued in 2020 describes injection into the remaining irradiated submandibular gland under local

anesthesia approximately four weeks after radiotherapy [NEW: UMCG participant information].

The registered primary outcomes are adverse events during the first year and salivary flow at 6

and 12 months, with secondary assessments including patient-reported outcomes and oncological

follow-up extending to five years [NEW: NCT04593589].



                                               31
  The published manufacturing process includes cryopreservation of an intermediate cell

product, subsequent expansion, and release testing for viability, sterility and endotoxins22. The

specified range of 5 × 10⁴–5 × 10⁶ cells per vial represents a final-product manufacturing

specification rather than a confirmed participant-level administered dose. The final formulated

product has a reported refrigerated shelf life of four hours, distinct from the storage of

cryopreserved intermediate cells22. Clinical outcomes from RESTART have not yet been publicly

reported.

  Together, efforts in Europe, South Korea and North America indicate growing momentum

rather than definitive efficacy. Evidence for graft persistence and functional contribution remains

limited to relatively short-term preclinical observations. Human salivary organoids transplanted

orthotopically into NOD mice were assessed at seven weeks, with evidence of host ductal

integration and human-specific salivary proteins²¹. In a separate radiation-injury model, murine

organoid allografts improved salivary secretion through eight weeks after transplantation,

although donor-cell detection varied among recipients⁷⁷. Human organoid grafts beneath the

renal capsule formed gland-like structures over twelve weeks, but this ectopic transplantation site

cannot demonstrate drainage into the oral cavity²¹. Together, these findings support regenerative

feasibility without establishing durable, patent ductal drainage and sustained secretion in human

recipients.

  Autologous approaches reduce donor–recipient alloimmune mismatch but require

individualized tissue procurement, expansion, testing and product release. The optimized

RESTART process involves approximately 14–20 days of post-thaw culture, excluding tissue

procurement and quality-control activities, while cryopreservation of intermediate products

provides scheduling flexibility²². Banked allogeneic products could shorten patient waiting times

and facilitate larger-scale manufacture but introduce donor selection and banking requirements

as well as potential alloimmune rejection. Comparative manufacturing costs, lead times and



                                                32
clinical outcomes for human salivary organoid therapies remain unestablished²²,[N4].

Alloimmune rejection arising from donor–recipient incompatibility and recurrent autoimmunity

in SjD represent distinct barriers: even autologous epithelial grafts may remain vulnerable to a

persistently inflammatory host microenvironment²⁰,¹³⁹. In autoimmune-prone NOD mice, human

organoid transplantation improved salivary secretion at seven weeks without reducing

lymphocytic focus scores²¹, demonstrating short-term functional benefit despite ongoing

inflammation but not long-term immune protection. Similarly, human organoid xenografts in

immunodeficient recipients and murine organoid transplantation after irradiation do not establish

long-term immune compatibility in human recipients²¹,⁷⁷. Potential strategies include donor–

recipient compatibility assessment for allogeneic products and local or disease-directed

immunomodulation in autoimmune recipients, although these approaches remain unvalidated for

salivary organoid graft protection. Future studies should assess donor-cell persistence, graft-

associated immune infiltration, epithelial injury and salivary function longitudinally in disease-

relevant models.

  Significant challenges remain: manufacturing is labor-intensive, demands rigorous GMP

control and still depends in many protocols on complex matrices or open aseptic steps, raising

scalability and regulatory concerns22,77. Whether regenerated epithelium can form functional

drainage connections to the oral cavity is unresolved, as is whether transplanted cells achieve

durable engraftment and sustained secretion in humans21,22. Early trial outcomes will therefore be

decisive in defining therapeutic durability, safety, manufacturing feasibility and the regulatory

pathway. Regenerative translation thus brings the three constraints together: sufficiently mature

secretory cells, functional integration with the host gland and reproducible manufacture with

established safety.



Chemical Reprogramming Culture and Epithelial Progenitor Cell Therapy



                                               33
A central bottleneck for progenitor cell therapy is expanding human salivary epithelial cells to

clinical scale: conventional 2D cultures senesce and cannot be maintained, while 3D organoids

scale less efficiently and consume substantial matrix and growth factors. Chemical

reprogramming culture (CRC) addresses this by using a defined small-molecule cocktail to

expand human salivary basal progenitor cells (SG-BPCs) in feeder- and Matrigel-free 2D

culture79. The strategy belongs to a broader class in which small molecules, rather than

exogenous transcription factors, control human cell fate, and the same modality has been used

to reprogram somatic cells to pluripotency and convert fibroblasts into functional epithelial

cells for organ repair, establishing chemical reprogramming as a non-integrative, controllable

route to therapeutic cells170,171.

  Mechanistically, CRC is grounded in niche-relevant signaling control. With the ROCK

inhibitor Y-27632, the TGF-β inhibitor A83-01 and the BMP inhibitor LDN193189, dual TGF-

β/BMP inhibition suppresses differentiation and sustains the basal progenitor state while

ROCK inhibition limits senescence and apoptosis79. The same dual TGF-β/BMP inhibition

module (A83-01 with LDN193189) forms the foundational cocktail for reprogramming

fibroblasts into retinal pigment epithelium, underscoring that these modules transfer across

epithelial systems171. Under these conditions, salivary epithelial cells surpassed 50 population

doublings in roughly 80 days, exceeding the Hayflick limit that constrained cells grown without

the molecules. High-passage karyotyping detected no gross chromosomal alterations in the

cultures examined, although this does not exclude submicroscopic genomic changes or

establish tumorigenic safety; the cocktail selectively enriched basal ductal progenitors retaining

KRT5, KRT19 and SOX9 with a predominantly CD49f⁺/CD26⁻ phenotype rather than

terminally differentiated luminal cells79.

  This 2D platform complements rather than replaces 3D organoid culture. The expanded SG-

BPCs retained organoid-forming capacity and, when transferred to 3D culture, differentiated



                                               34
toward acinar (AQP5, AMY1, BHLHA15) and myoepithelial (ACTA2) lineages, so organoid

differentiation serves as a functional-validation step 79. This defines a coherent pipeline: CRC

provides large-scale progenitor expansion, organoid differentiation confirms lineage potential

and function, and the expanded cells are applied as a product. Consistent with this,

administration of SG-BPCs into a mouse model of radiation-induced hypofunction restored

salivary function79, mirroring outcomes reported for other chemically reprogrammed epithelial

cells that integrate into host tissue and restore organ function171.

  This epithelial-progenitor approach is conceptually distinct from mesenchymal stromal cell

(MSC) therapy. Whereas MSC paracrine effects are not tissue-specific and may incompletely

target salivary epithelium, SG-BPCs offer tissue-specific differentiation with lineage-

appropriate paracrine potential. Because CRC is chemically defined, feeder-free and non-

integrative, it avoids exogenous transcription-factor delivery and a pluripotent intermediate,

but these features alone do not establish tumorigenic safety. No dedicated in vivo teratoma

formation or tumorigenicity assessment has been reported for these SG-BPCs, and the mouse

efficacy study evaluated outcomes only up to eight weeks after cell administration⁷⁹. Although

teratoma testing primarily addresses the presence of pluripotent cells, expanded tissue-

restricted progenitors still require appropriate assessment of abnormal or ectopic growth,

passage-associated genetic alterations and long-term safety. Chemical reprogramming is

therefore an attractive translational strategy, although clinical-scale manufacturing, potency

testing and long-term safety remain to be established 170-172. CRC-based epithelial progenitor

therapy is best viewed as a potentially scalable, tissue-specific complement to organoid

transplantation.



Extracellular Vesicle Strategies: Targeted Cell-Free Therapy

Beyond cell transplantation, extracellular vesicles (EVs) offer a cell-free strategy, with direct



                                                35
salivary gland evidence from gland- or saliva-derived EV preparations evaluated in organoid

assays and/or preclinical gland disease models¹⁶⁶,¹⁷⁹,¹⁸⁰. More broadly, EVs from stem cells

isolated from adipose tissue, urine and dental pulp, as well as from iPSCs, have shown anti-

fibrotic and immunomodulatory activity in related models 173-176. These products derive from

non-salivary source cells and should be distinguished from salivary gland-derived EVs. CD9-

enriched EVs from chemically reprogrammed basal progenitors exert anti-fibrotic effects in

human salivary organoids and a mouse duct-obstruction model by delivering miR-3162 and

miR-1290 to suppress ACVR1 signaling, with microfluidic chip-based purification and ductal

delivery offering a minimally invasive route to the affected gland 166. From an endogenous

source, saliva-derived EVs attenuated TGF-β-induced fibrogenic activity in human salivary

fibroblasts and organoids through STAT3 modulation177, while EVs from salivary stem cells

cultured on WNT3A-loaded microwell scaffolds conferred radioprotection in irradiated human

salivary organoids and mouse glands via YWHAZ enrichment and PI3K–AKT activation in

recipient cells, showing how biomaterial-defined niches tune EV cargo178. Unlike transplanted

cells, EVs do not require persistent engraftment, but their clinical development still depends

on product-specific potency, dose and manufacturing controls. Potency assays should measure

biological activity relevant to the intended mechanism, such as attenuation of profibrotic

signaling in salivary gland organoids or fibroblasts, rather than relying on EV identity markers

or cargo abundance alone¹⁶⁶,¹⁷⁷. Dose reporting should include particle and protein

measurements alongside functional activity, without treating any single measure as a

universally validated dosing unit. Route-dependent gland retention, biodistribution, clearance

and repeat-dose exposure require further evaluation. Manufacturing scale-up must ensure

comparability across source-cell batches, EV isolation, formulation and storage while

controlling non-vesicular contaminants and microbial impurities. Minimal Information for

FUTURE PERSPECTIVES AND EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES

Across the applications reviewed above, developmental maturity determines the range of

physiological functions that organoids can reproduce, multicellular organization determines

which tissue-level interactions they can capture, and standardization determines whether

experimental advances can be translated into reliable clinical applications. These

interdependent constraints motivate the following approaches to acinar differentiation,

multicellular integration, bioengineering and clinical standardization.

Overcoming the Acinar Differentiation Barrier

A challenge that cuts across many applications of human salivary gland organoids is their

incomplete and protocol-dependent differentiation toward mature acinar lineages13,21,77. Current

strategies, including Notch inhibition, ALK/TGF-β modulation, FGF-based mesenchymal cues,

and stromal or mesenchymal co-culture, have made incremental progress, but native-level human

acinar maturation and durable secretory output are not yet consistently achieved across culture

systems13,18,21,89,182. Future directions include identifying acinar-specific transcription-factor

networks through single-cell multi-omics, developing temporally controlled protocols that

sequentially activate progenitor expansion and acinar-commitment programs, and engineering

culture environments that supply the mechanical and biochemical cues acinar maturation

requires2,21,36. Incorporating cellular aging is a further consideration: age-associated changes such

as senescent-cell accumulation and declining regenerative capacity are likely to influence

organoid initiation, differentiation potential and regenerative performance, yet remain



                                                 37
insufficiently modeled23,146,149.


Multicellular System Integration

The future of the field lies in more sophisticated models that incorporate the native tissue's neural,

stromal, vascular, immune and extracellular-matrix compartments, which today's epithelial-only

systems lack36,135,147,182. Direct experimental evidence for stromal and vascular regulation comes

primarily from developmental models. In murine embryonic salivary organoids, PDGFRα⁺

stromal cells promoted proacinar differentiation through FGF2-responsive signaling involving

BMP2 and BMP7¹⁸. In embryonic mouse salivary gland explants and reconstituted tissues,

endothelial-cell depletion or VEGFR2 inhibition disrupted epithelial patterning and altered

ductal and progenitor populations¹⁹. These findings demonstrate instructive roles for non-

epithelial cells, although their contributions to mature human organoid secretion remain

incompletely defined.

        Neuronal integration presents both technical challenges and opportunities. Salivary

glands are densely innervated by sympathetic and parasympathetic neurons that regulate

secretion through complex neurotransmitter signaling1, and incorporating peripheral neurons or

neural components derived from organoids could enable studies of neuroepithelial interaction,

autonomic regulation, neurogenic dysfunction and gland-specific pharmacology36,47,162. Proof-of-

concept studies include engineered salivary-like constructs derived from human dental pulp

stem cells, which exhibited neurostimulation-responsive calcium signaling and amylase

production in vitro and stimulated epithelial and neuronal growth in an ex vivo damaged-gland

model⁹⁰, as well as murine SGO–N2a co-cultures showing BoNT-responsive changes in

secretory markers162. However, these findings do not establish stable, physiologically patterned

autonomic innervation or long-term neurosecretory regulation following human organoid

transplantation. The recent identification of TUBB3/VIM double-positive neural-like cells in

chemically defined organoid cultures suggests that some conditions may support neural-like


                                                 38
stromal elements. If these prove to participate in neurosecretory signaling, they could reduce

reliance on exogenous neuronal supplementation21.

        Vascular integration is essential for modeling drug exposure, hypoxic responses, immune

trafficking and regeneration21,147. Perfused microphysiological systems and salivary tissue-chip

platforms can provide controlled exposure, support high-content functional assays and improve

modeling of radiation response and drug screening relative to static cultures147, but faithful

reconstruction of ductal flow, vascular perfusion and obstructive salivary disease physiology

remains an unresolved engineering challenge147,183.

        Tumor microenvironment modeling with multicellular organoids is a compelling cancer

application. Incorporating cancer-associated fibroblasts, immune cells, endothelial cells and

matrix with tumor organoids may yield more accurate models of therapeutic responses, immune

evasion and resistance184,185, and patient-derived stromal and immune cells combined with tumor

organoids could enable personalized microenvironment models that capture individual variation

in tumor–stroma and tumor–immune interactions, although salivary gland cancer-specific

implementations remain limited16,101,113.


Bioengineering Solutions

3D bioprinting enables organoid or tissue-engineered structures with greater spatial control than

self-organizing spheroids alone 27,186. Precisely positioning epithelial, stromal, vascular and

neural components could reconstruct branched acinar–ductal architecture and support more

physiological cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions, but fabricating fully functional,

transplantable constructs remains a long-term goal rather than a clinical reality27,183. Ultimately,

combining bioprinting with organoid technology may enable implantable glandular constructs

with defined architecture, vascular support and functional drainage, provided maturation,

integration and safety barriers are overcome.

        Beyond direct bioprinting, scaffold-guided spheroid engineering offers a complementary


                                                39
strategy for controlling spheroid formation, phenotype, retrieval and delivery. A

thermoresponsive fiber-based microwell scaffold supported uniform salivary gland stem-cell

(SGSC) spheroid formation and enabled nonenzymatic retrieval by lowering the temperature187.

Compared with monolayer cultures, scaffold-generated spheroids showed increased expression

of stemness-associated genes, including LGR4, LGR5 and LGR6, and enhanced paracrine

growth-factor expression187. Following implantation into irradiation-damaged mouse salivary

glands, they reduced tissue injury, preserved acinar and ductal structures and promoted

recovery of secretory function187. Building on this approach, a WNT3A-releasing double-

layered microwell scaffold provided sustained biochemical stimulation during spheroid culture

and increased EV production; the resulting EVs showed regenerative effects in irradiated

human salivary organoids and a mouse model of radiation injury 178. Together, these studies

illustrate how engineered scaffolds can serve not only as structural platforms but also as

bioactive systems for producing functionally optimized cell- and EV-based products. These

findings remain preclinical and do not establish therapeutic efficacy in human recipients.

              Automated culture systems and robotic platforms address scalability and standardization
123,188-190
          . They can perform routine maintenance, media exchange, passaging, drug screening,

imaging and analysis with greater consistency than manual workflows, while artificial

intelligence and high-content imaging can assist quality control and phenotypic analysis188,190.

For salivary gland organoids, such automation will be particularly important for reducing

operator-dependent variability, shortening PDO screening timelines, and enabling GMP-

compatible manufacturing of therapeutic cell products, as well as standardized production of

scaffold-derived spheroids and EV-based products22,189.



Standardization and Clinical Translation

The clinical translation of salivary gland organoid technologies requires standardization



                                                    40
frameworks that remain underdeveloped for this organ system 11,22,23. Methodological

variability among laboratories in tissue processing, media formulation, matrix composition,

passaging, differentiation conditions and functional assessment criteria makes cross-study

comparison of organoid phenotypes and drug sensitivity data difficult 16,22,77,123, so consensus

protocols, standardized reference materials, shared reporting criteria and interlaboratory

validation remain priorities for the reproducibility that scientific and regulatory communities

require11,123,179. For precision oncology, this means defined benchmarks for tumor-cell

authentication, minimum criteria for genomic and histological fidelity to parental tumors, and

harmonized screening at clinically relevant concentrations, with turnaround shortened to align

with clinical decision-making16,101,113. Research-grade standardization primarily supports

reproducibility, whereas clinical-grade manufacture of therapeutic cell products additionally

requires GMP-compliant processes, traceability and product-specific release criteria²²,[N4]

(Table 6).

       Regulatory pathways differ by application. Decision-support tests such as PDO drug

sensitivity testing may fall within regulatory frameworks governing laboratory-developed tests

(LDTs), in vitro diagnostic (IVD) tests or clinical laboratory services, with requirements

varying by jurisdiction and intended use. Analytical performance, clinical validity and clinical

utility should therefore be evaluated as distinct considerations123. In the United States, Clinical

Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA) requirements address laboratory quality but do

not themselves establish clinical utility [N7]. The 2024 LDT rule issued by the U.S. Food and

Drug Administration (FDA) was vacated in 2025, and the regulatory text was subsequently

reverted [N8]. In the European Union, tests with an IVD intended purpose are subject to the In

Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (IVDR), including the conditional health-

institution exemption under Article 5(5) [N9].

       Therapeutic applications involving organoid-derived cells require product- and



                                                41
jurisdiction-specific oversight, which may include the European Union’s advanced therapy

medicinal product (ATMP) framework22,[N4]. Product-specific quality and release criteria

should address identity, purity, potency and sterility, whereas tumorigenicity and

biodistribution are evaluated during product development, with risk-based long-term clinical

follow-up [N4,N12]. For EV products, MISEV2023 provides a consensus framework for EV

characterization and reporting but does not constitute a regulatory lot-release assay 179.

Functional pharmacology applications such as BoNT assays require product-specific validation

and benchmarking against established potency assays156,158,159. Studies using murine salivary

organoid–neuronal co-cultures have demonstrated BoNT-responsive changes in secretory

markers¹⁶², but these assays have not yet been incorporated into pharmacopoeial monographs.

        Addressing current manufacturing limitations will require convergence of chemically

defined matrices that replace xenogeneic substrates, closed or semi-closed automated culture

that ensures batch consistency, and prospective registry studies capturing long-term functional

outcomes21,22,77. Experience from the Dutch first-in-human program, South Korean preclinical

platforms and the US salivary tissue-organoid biorepository will help shape practical

manufacturing benchmarks and regulatory standards, although clinical efficacy still requires

prospective demonstration22,23,77,169.



CONCLUSION

Salivary gland organoid technology has reached a translational inflection point. What began as

exploratory efforts to culture glandular tissue in three dimensions has matured into a field with

early first-in-human regenerative translation22,169, patient-derived tumor organoid libraries16,17,

functional pharmacology platforms162, and emerging autoimmune and radiation-injury

models20,77,147.

        Organoid generation from multiple cell sources, guided by defined signaling



                                                42
dependencies across the WNT/R-spondin, FGF, EGF/NRG1, Notch and TGF-β/ALK pathways,

has produced versatile tools for basic and translational work. Yet the persistent ductal- or

basal/progenitor-dominant phenotype of many human systems remains a fundamental limitation

for functional and regenerative applications; chemically defined culture, Notch and ALK/TGF-

β modulation, and stromal cues have narrowed this gap without closing it 13,21. Characterizing

species-specific differences has clarified the complementary roles of each system, with murine

organoids supporting studies of morphogenesis, secretory physiology, injury and development,

and human organoids supporting patient-specific biology, precision medicine and translation13,77.

        The histological and molecular diversity of salivary gland tumors makes patient-derived

organoids especially valuable, since organoids from rare subtypes that lack representative cell

lines fill a critical preclinical gap. For non-neoplastic disease, patient-derived SjD organoids have

helped characterize epithelial-intrinsic dysfunction and interferon/JAK–STAT activation,

whereas future immune-integrated assembloids could extend this work to immune–epithelial

crosstalk20,135. Existing organoids isolate epithelial programs and report drug responses such as

JAK inhibition, while single-cell and spatial profiling identifies immune and stromal components

that future chimeric models could incorporate¹³⁹. These prospective extensions require direct

validation in human salivary disease models before their contributions to pathogenesis can be

established.

        Functional pharmacology platforms extend organoids beyond disease modeling.

Organoid-based BoNT assessment exemplifies this, offering a gland-specific neuroepithelial

assay that complements established neuronal potency assays. Although salivary organoids are

not yet suitable as regulatory release assays, their applicability to cholinergic agonist and

secretagogue screening, neurosecretory pharmacodynamics and the evaluation of complex

biologics such as EVs positions them as candidate platforms for future industrial and regulatory

development under the 3R framework, subject to rigorous validation156,162.



                                                 43
        Regenerative applications are advancing along complementary routes. Clinical

translation is emerging but unproven: the Dutch first-in-human program, South Korean collagen-

based transplantation and United States biorepository efforts signal momentum rather than

efficacy22,23,77,169. Chemical reprogramming culture is well suited to this pipeline because its

feeder- and Matrigel-free, small-molecule-defined format supports prolonged expansion of

tissue-specific progenitors that retain acinar and myoepithelial potential79 and may facilitate

scalable manufacturing. It may complement MSC- and iPSC-based approaches without requiring

a pluripotent intermediate. Cell-free EV strategies may further broaden therapeutic options,

although they remain largely preclinical166.

        Significant challenges remain: functional maturation beyond ductal- or basal/progenitor-

dominant states, the scalability of GMP-compliant manufacturing that still relies on complex

matrices and labor-intensive handling, the batch-to-batch variability of advanced multicellular

models, and the need for long-term safety and efficacy data from early clinical programs.

Addressing these interconnected developmental, architectural and translational challenges will

require sustained collaboration among researchers, clinicians, regulators, industry and patient

communities21,22.

        By analyzing species-specific organoid biology, tumor-subtype-specific precision

oncology, autoimmune and radiation-injury modeling, functional pharmacology, regenerative

transplantation and cell-free EV strategies in depth, this review has aimed to complement broader

surveys of oral and maxillofacial organoids with the specificity that salivary gland challenges

demand. How rapidly the technology reaches patients who currently have few effective options

will depend on the outcomes of ongoing translational programs and on continued refinement of

acinar differentiation, multicellular culture, standardized manufacturing and mechanism-specific

cell-free therapies.

Figure 2. Network of signaling pathways regulating organoid development.

The diagram illustrates the major molecular signals involved in organoid expansion,

maintenance, branching, and lineage differentiation. (1) Wnt/β-catenin signaling promotes

proliferation through WNT3A or the GSK3β inhibitor CHIR99021. (2) R-spondin 1 potentiates

Wnt signaling through LGR4, LGR5, and LGR6. (3) FGF7 and FGF10 activate FGFR2b-

dependent ERK and AKT signaling to promote branching morphogenesis and counterbalance

Wnt activity. (4) EGF-family ligands, including EGF, HB-EGF, and NRG1, activate MAPK

and PI3K–AKT signaling to support organoid growth and survival. (5) Inhibition of BMP

signaling by Noggin or dorsomorphin and inhibition of TGF-β signaling by A83-01 or

SB431542 help preserve epithelial identity and limit EMT and fibrosis. (6) Notch signaling

contributes to epithelial lineage regulation. DAPT inhibits NICD formation and is used in

differentiation protocols that can promote acinar-marker expression.
