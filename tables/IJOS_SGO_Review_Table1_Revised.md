# Table 1. Cell Sources for Salivary Gland Organoids

| Cell source | Accessibility & expansion | Key advantages | Key limitations | Applications | Refs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult epithelial cells | Readily accessible; salivary-lineage biased; limited expansion affected by age or pathology | Autologous use; native gland identity | No single defined stem-cell hierarchy; variable establishment and differentiation | Disease modeling; regeneration; drug screening | 13–15, 23, 30–32, 79, 88 |
| Pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs/ESCs) | iPSCs from somatic cells; ESCs from embryos; extensive expansion; directed differentiation required | Genetic and rare-disease modeling; potential access to major-gland lineages | Incomplete mature function; lengthy and costly differentiation; protocol-dependent engineering | Developmental and genetic disease modeling; viral tropism | 33–40, 47 |
| Embryonic and fetal tissues | Limited availability; abundant native progenitors; high proliferation and differentiation potential | Robust branching morphogenesis; benchmarks for PSC differentiation | Ethical and supply constraints; incomplete ductal architecture | Developmental biology; reference standards | 41–46 |

An integrated approach matching each cell source to the best-suited research question provides the most comprehensive platform.

Abbreviations: iPSC, induced pluripotent stem cell; ESC, embryonic stem cell; PSC, pluripotent stem cell.
