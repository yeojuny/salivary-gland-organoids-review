# Table 2. Murine versus Human Salivary Gland Organoids

| Feature | Murine organoids | Human organoids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acinar differentiation | Acinar-like differentiation achievable under selected conditions; extent varies by gland and protocol. | Often ductal/basal-enriched during expansion; acinar differentiation remains protocol- and niche-dependent. |
| Typical morphology | Lumenized, cystic or branched structures; acinar-like clusters in some protocols. | Cystic, solid/dense or branched structures, depending on source and culture conditions. |
| Lineages / markers | Acinar, ductal and myoepithelial features reported; AQP5, BHLHA15/MIST1, AMY, mucins. | KRT5/KRT14 basal and KRT7/KRT19 ductal features; AQP5, BHLHA15 and AMY induction varies. |
| Secretory function | Secretagogue-responsive calcium signals, swelling and amylase activity reported in selected models. | Calcium and swelling responses demonstrated; mature secretory output remains variable and incompletely benchmarked. |
| ECM / media requirements | Outcomes depend on gland source, medium, passage and ECM conditions, including batch effects. | Sensitive to donor/cell source, medium, ECM and the timing of differentiation cues. |
| Stem/progenitor plasticity | Lineage contributions and plasticity reported in defined developmental and injury settings. | Progenitor-like states and differentiation reported; matched cross-species comparisons remain limited. |
| Donor / model variability | Inbred strains can reduce variation; strain, age, gland and protocol still influence outcomes. | Donor age, gland source, pathology, prior injury and culture history contribute to heterogeneity. |
| Representative applications | Development, morphogenesis, lineage regulation, injury–repair and secretory physiology. | Patient-specific disease and cancer models, drug testing and regenerative translation. |
| Refs | 13,14,61,77,96 | 13,21,23,77,79,89 |
